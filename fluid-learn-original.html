Case study · StudySmarter

Monetisation experiments

Fluid Learn mode is StudySmarter's most engaging way to study, and it costs money every time it runs. I designed and tested ways to put it behind Premium without hurting how much people learn.

Role
Product designer
Platform
Mobile and web
Method
A/B/n test, 3 variants

Impact

  • +33%conversion to a paid subscription
  • 0%change in the key engagement check, learning days with 5+ minutes
  • +5–10%projected lift in lifetime value

A mode that was a financial liability became a revenue driver.

Fluid Learn mode (FLM)

Fluid Learn mode helps learners convert study material into a spaced-repetition based quiz, with a variety of question types.

Research & insights

Business POV

Why monetise this mode?

  • Opportunity

    FLM has emerged as a highly engaging learning experience, highlighting its potential as an untapped monetisation opportunity.

  • Financial liability

    AI-generated questions have variable costs, and increased user engagement raises financial exposure without matching revenue.

  • Strategy

    High-level strategy is to associate high-value AI capabilities with our Premium tier for sustainable business growth.

Understanding users

We surveyed 105 users and looked into previous research to understand the appetite for monetisation.

  • Value proposition

    Users value AI when it saves time or delivers better output than competitors (e.g. ChatGPT).

  • Friction points

    Credit-based systems were identified as stressful and confusing, and 44% of users preferred unlimited subscription access.

  • Fragmented premium logic

    Each feature had its own free limit, so it was hard to see what Premium actually gave you. For example, free users got 9 AI assistant messages, 2 study sets and 3 podcasts, each capped separately.

Design process

Problem statement

Fluid Learn Mode is a high-engagement, AI-powered feature that has become a financial liability due to its variable generation costs.

This user engagement needs to be translated into the sustainable unit economics required for scaling.

Design approach

Multi-variant testing

We wanted to test the impact of different monetisation models, so we decided to run an A/B/n test (several versions against a control group) to measure the effectiveness of each.

  • Control

    Unlimited Fluid Learn mode.

  • Treatment 1

    Feature-limited access.

  • Treatment 2

    Daily session charges.

Key metrics

We tracked a primary conversion metric against key "guardrail" engagement metrics (numbers that must not get worse). The goal was to prove that a shift toward monetisation would not significantly impact engagement.

  • Measuring success

    Conversion to paid

    Conversion from starting FLM to paid subscription within 10 days.

  • Guardrail

    Engagement

    Conversion to learning day with 5+ minutes.

Key design question

How might we

monetise Fluid Learn Mode to ensure sustainable growth, while positioning Premium as an expansion of the learning experience rather than a barrier to it?

Design explorations

Some of the choices I made, before settling on two treatments to test.

1 Concept 1: Lesson-based monetisation

I proposed replacing mode-specific limits with a unified "Key" system, granting users two daily keys to unlock any lesson for use in any mode.

This strategic shift was postponed to prevent disrupting the ongoing course page redesign.

2 Concept 2: Limit-based monetisation

I explored multiple limit-based mechanics that incentivised users to come back daily:

  • Hearts (mistake-based): penalises errors and discourages exploration, which runs counter to our goal of building learner confidence.
  • Charges (session-based): provides a transparent, predictable limit that focuses on session completion rather than mistake avoidance.

3 Concept 3: Freemium

I also explored a freemium model providing universal access to Fluid Learn Mode, while gating "power-up" features such as hints and explanations.

  • Free access to practice lets users establish consistent study habits before they meet conversion triggers.
  • Upsells can be triggered at the right time, when a user wants support through explanations or hints.

T1 Treatment 1: Feature-limited access

After three unrestricted sessions, users retain access to core learning, but face feature limitations such as restricted question types, locked hints and disabled explanations.

T2 Treatment 2: Daily session charges

Users are granted two daily charges that refresh every 24 hours. When depleted, they are prompted to upgrade or pivot to another learning mode. While this model incentivises users to come back daily, it blocks usage of the feature entirely.

Rollout & impact

Results

Results · T1: Feature-limited access

  • +33%Conversion to paid
  • 0%Conversion to learning day with 5+ mins

We saw a drop in some engagement metrics, like the percentage of time spent in learning modes, and a significant increase in conversion to paid subscription. Conversion to a learning day with 5+ minutes, the key guardrail metric, did not change. Projected +5–10% LTV (lifetime value) uplift.

Results · T2: Daily session charge

  • –Conversion to paid
  • –Conversion to learning day with 5+ mins

One week after launch it was underperforming significantly, so all traffic was routed to Treatment 1 and control.

Next steps

Treatment 1, feature-limited access, was a clear winner. Next steps are to roll it out to existing users:

  • Introduce a one-off modal the first time existing users start an FLM session, to clearly communicate the new limits.
  • Continue iterating on free-user limitations to balance Premium revenue and learning engagement.
